Call center and 24/7 operation cleaning

Continuous cleaning that interrupts neither the operation nor the focus.

A call center runs on rotating shifts, with staff at fixed stations for hours. The environment degrades fast: shared headsets, desks in constant use, high-turnover break areas. Cleaning has to happen in parallel with the operation, without excessive noise, without strong-smelling chemicals and without interrupting live calls.

This page is about what is specific to continuous operations. The daily office program, with zones, frequencies and reporting, is at corporate cleaning.

How does cleaning change by type of continuous operation?

An operation that never closes is not cleaned the same way in every area.

The call center floor

Rows of stations occupied for hours, with equipment, headsets and plenty of contact with surfaces. It is cleaned in short blocks, without pulling anyone from their seat, and with mild-smelling products.

Data centers and NOC rooms

These are technical rooms with limited access and sensitive equipment. Which zones are touched, with which products and with whom present is agreed with the client before starting, and no liquids are used near the equipment.

Back office and shared service centers

Administrative operations with steadier shifts, where cleaning resembles that of a large office and can be scheduled by zone and hour.

Break areas, kitchens and high-turnover restrooms

These are where wear concentrates: in a rotating-shift operation there is never a moment without use. They are attended more often and in rounds.

How is cleaning done without interrupting calls or focus?

With low noise, products without strong odors and work in short blocks, so no zone is worked on for long. The round is coordinated with floor supervisors to avoid call peaks.

If the client asks, station cleaning is done between shifts, when the station is free for a few minutes, rather than with the agent present.

How are headsets and shared peripherals sanitized?

Shared stations, with headsets, keyboards, mice and phones, are the point of most contact on the floor. The working rule is to sanitize between one shift and the next, with products compatible with electronic equipment and without spraying onto it, using barely damp cloths.

Ear cushions and other personal-use accessories depend on each operation's policy, and it is worth setting out in writing what is sanitized on site and what is replaced.

How is cleaning coordinated with the client's rotating shifts?

Cleaning is organized around the client's shifts: each cleaning team knows the operation's handover time and avoids working right at the change. With fixed teams for each site, staff know the floor, the schedules and the critical points.

Each shift leaves a report of what was carried out, so the client's supervisor can see what was done on their shift without having to accompany the team.

How are weekends, holidays and absences covered?

A continuous operation does not close on holidays, so the cleaning calendar is agreed for every day of the year. Absences and replacements are handled through staff outsourcing, so a shift is not left uncovered.
