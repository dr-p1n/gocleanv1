Corporate building cleaning

The building's image is the first impression of every company inside it.

A corporate building with dull lobbies, neglected restrooms or stained floors communicates negligence before the visitor even reaches the tenant's floor. For property managers, cleaning is tenant retention: every unresolved complaint is an argument not to renew.

This page is about common areas and how a building with several tenants is coordinated. If your company occupies an office inside the building, see office cleaning; the daily program, with zones and reporting, is at corporate cleaning.

Which zones of a corporate building need the most attention?

A building brings together very different spaces, and each is cleaned by its own criteria.

Lobby and reception

This is the building's first impression. Floor, glass, front desk and furniture have to be spotless from opening time, and they are checked through the day because foot traffic keeps dirtying them.

Elevators and stairwells

These are the most-touched points in the building: buttons, handrails, walls and the cab floor. They call for frequent disinfection of what gets touched and attention to the floor, where the dirt that comes in from the street builds up.

Common restrooms on each floor

They are in constant use and the source of some of tenants' most frequent complaints. They are attended several times through the day, with supplies replenished, and are not left for closing time.

Parking and rooftops

These are service areas with a different kind of dirt: dust, oil, leaves and debris. They are scheduled on their own frequency, more spread out than indoor areas.

Who cleans what: the common areas or the tenants' floors?

In a building with several tenants, two responsibilities coexist. Building management contracts the common areas, such as the lobby, elevators, common restrooms and parking, and each company contracts the cleaning of its own floor.

Problems arise at the boundary: the floor door, the corridor on the floor or the restroom two tenants share. It is worth setting out in writing where one contract ends and the other begins, so that no zone is left without someone responsible. A company that occupies whole floors can handle them with its own corporate cleaning program.

How is cleaning coordinated with building management?

Through direct coordination with building management and a report for each shift that records what was carried out. Go Services assigns permanent teams to each building, so the people cleaning know the areas and the schedules of the site.

That report serves an audit, and it serves when a tenant complains, because it shows what was done, in which zone and when.

How is the building cleaned without disturbing tenants and visitors?

The building is never empty: some tenants arrive early, visitors come through the day and some companies leave late. So the most exposed areas, such as the lobby and elevators, are cleaned before opening and checked during the day, and floor-by-floor cleaning follows a schedule adapted to office hours.

When a floor is wet or a service elevator is being worked on, the zone is marked off and the access that building management defines is used, so nobody has to step around the work.

What does cleaning facades and exterior glass at height involve?

It is different work from daily cleaning. Work at height calls for its own equipment and procedures, depends on the weather and is scheduled as an intervention with its own calendar, not as part of the daily service. The detail is under windows and facades.
