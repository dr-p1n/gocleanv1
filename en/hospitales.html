Hospital and healthcare cleaning

Where cleaning is part of the medical protocol, not an auxiliary service.

In a hospital, a poorly disinfected surface can become a vector for hospital-acquired infection. Clinical cleaning is not aesthetic, it is epidemiological. Health facilities face Ministry of Health audits, accreditations and the direct responsibility of protecting immunocompromised patients.

This page is for whoever runs a health facility and has to decide how to contract its cleaning. The technical detail of the protocols, with areas, terminal disinfection and waste, is at hospital cleaning and clinical biosafety.

How does the type of health facility change what you need?

Facilities do not share the same risk or the same pace, and what you contract changes with them.

Hospitals and clinics with inpatient beds

They have critical areas, rooms that turn over with every discharge, and bio-infectious waste every day. Cleaning is continuous and runs in shifts, and what weighs most when contracting is terminal disinfection and the traceability of each intervention.

Polyclinics and outpatient health centers

They see a high volume of patients in a short time, above all in waiting rooms and consulting rooms. The focus is on high-touch surfaces and on cleaning between appointments without slowing care down.

Laboratories and diagnostic centers

They handle samples and, depending on the area, sensitive equipment. Staff need to know which zones are cleaned and which equipment is left alone, and how the biological waste from sample collection is managed.

Specialist clinics and consulting offices

Smaller spaces, with one or a few procedure rooms, such as dentistry or dermatology. They need a clear protocol for each room and an agreed frequency, without the structure of a hospital.

What should a health facility ask of its cleaning provider?

Beyond price, these are the criteria that separate a provider ready for a clinical setting from one that simply cleans:

  • Staff on the formal payroll, with background checks
  • Training in biosafety and in the use of personal protective equipment
  • A plan by area according to risk level, not one uniform service for the whole facility
  • A record of every intervention and a report for each shift that the facility can audit
  • A fixed team assigned to the site, with on-site supervision
  • A clear way to cover absences without leaving an area unattended
  • Products with a technical data sheet available

Go Services works with formal-payroll staff with background checks, fixed teams for each facility, and auditable checklists with a report for every shift. The guide to how to choose a cleaning company expands on these criteria for comparing proposals.

Should a hospital outsource its cleaning or keep it in-house?

It depends on the facility's capacity to hire, train, supervise and replace staff. With in-house staff, the facility controls the team directly, but it also takes on ongoing training, absences and supervision.

With a provider, that management shifts across and the facility keeps control through the contract: it requires protocols, records and supervision. It is also possible to combine both models, with in-house staff in the most sensitive areas and a provider for the rest. How this works is covered under staff outsourcing.

How does the service start in a health facility?

Start-up follows a sequence, so that the protocol exists before the first shift:

  • A walk-through of the site and classification of the areas by risk level
  • A protocol, checklists and frequency for each area, defined together with the facility's management
  • Assignment of the facility's fixed team
  • A supervised start and the first shift report

Is hospital cleaning the same as cleaning a health facility?

They share a base: disinfection according to risk level, waste handling and a record of what was done. But they answer different questions.

Hospital cleaning is the technical service: protocols by area, terminal disinfection and waste, covered in hospital cleaning and clinical biosafety. Cleaning a health facility is the client's decision: what type of facility it is, what risk each area carries and what service structure it needs.
