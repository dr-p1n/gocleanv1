School and campus cleaning

Safe, healthy environments for students and teaching staff.

In an educational facility, biosafety protects the most vulnerable population and reassures families. Clean, sanitized environments help contain contagion and keep the school year running. A school with visible cleaning protocols does not just comply: it stands out to the families deciding where to enroll.

If you run a school, a college or a university, this is what is worth knowing before you contract cleaning. What is cleaned in each zone, with which products and on what schedule is explained at cleaning for schools and educational centers.

How does the type of educational facility change what you need?

A preschool and a university are not cleaned the same way, and what you contract changes with the age of the students and the size of the campus.

Preschools and daycare centers

Small children who crawl, eat on the floor and put objects in their mouths. They call for more floor and toy disinfection, very low-toxicity products and special attention to restrooms and changing areas.

Elementary and secondary schools

This is the bulk of the demand: classrooms, cafeteria, restrooms and sports areas with heavy traffic and tight schedules. What weighs most is finishing before classes begin and agreeing in the contract how restrooms are kept up during the school day.

Universities and institutes

Campuses with several buildings, laboratories, libraries and common areas, and schedules that vary by faculty and by shift. Here what counts is coordinating schedules building by building and being able to cover several shifts.

Training centers and academies

Training rooms and workshops with afternoon and evening classes. They need a service that adapts to when classes are held, rather than to a fixed school calendar.

What should a school ask of its cleaning provider?

With minors on the premises, some criteria weigh more than price:

  • Background checks for every staff member who enters the grounds, and staff on the formal payroll
  • Low-toxicity products with a technical data sheet available
  • Working hours outside the school day, defined in writing
  • A fixed team assigned to the facility, so the staff are always the same people
  • Checklists by zone and a report for each shift that administration can review
  • A clear way to cover absences before classes begin

At Go Services staff are on the formal payroll and go through background checks, the biofriendly protocol is an included standard, and each facility has its own fixed team and auditable checklists. To carry these criteria into a comparison of proposals, there is a full guide to how to choose a cleaning company.

Should a school outsource its cleaning or keep its own custodians?

A school can keep its own custodians for the day-to-day, and that works for upkeep during the school day. What tends to stretch a small team is deep cleaning, absence cover and cleaning during breaks, when more people are needed for a few days.

A mixed model is possible: in-house custodians for the school day and a provider for deep cleaning and breaks. More on this model under staff outsourcing.

How does the service start in a school?

The best time is usually the break before classes begin, because it allows an initial deep clean without students on site. The sequence is:

  • A walk-through of the grounds and a map of the zones: classrooms, restrooms, cafeteria and sports areas
  • Checklists, products and schedules defined together with administration
  • An initial deep clean during a break, where possible
  • Assignment of the fixed team and a start with a report for each shift

How is the cleaning protocol communicated to families?

A cleaning protocol only sets a school apart if families know about it. Checklists and shift reports give administration concrete information to share with parents, instead of general promises.
