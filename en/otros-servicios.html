Additional facility services

The work that surrounds cleaning and also has to run every day.

A facility is not kept up by cleaning alone. The grounds keep growing, the mail keeps arriving, the trash has to go out, and someone has to be at the front desk when the doors open. Taken one at a time these are small jobs; added up they are a real load that usually ends up split among whoever has a free moment.

Go Services handles them as complementary services alongside the cleaning contract. They are not tasks bolted onto the cleaning shift: each one has its own schedule, its own staff and its own supervision, which is why they are defined separately even when the same provider carries them out.

Which complementary services does Go Services cover?

There are four distinct areas, and each is contracted against the facility's real workload, not as a generic add-on.

What they have in common is that none of them is solved by a one-off visit: they all call for presence at a defined frequency.

Grounds and green-area care

Mowing, clearing planters and removing yard waste at entrances, parking areas and courtyards. In Panama's climate the rainy season changes the rhythm: what is handled every two weeks in the dry season calls for more frequency in the rain. Pest control in gardens is contracted separately, under fumigation.

Concierge and reception

Staff at the entrance to receive visitors, log arrivals and handle the mail that comes in during the day. In a building with several tenants this is the post that sets the first impression, and its day looks a lot like the one described under buildings and common areas.

Waste management and recycling

Internal collection, separation at the point where the waste is generated, and transfer to the holding area so it goes out on collection day. What decides whether it works is not the service itself, but whether the separation points are where people actually throw things away.

Internal courier and operational support

Moving documents and materials between floors or between sites, plus the support tasks that come up during the day and have no fixed owner.

How are these services coordinated with daily operations?

Each one has a different time window, and that is half the work.

Grounds care is done early or on weekends, because it is noisy and kicks up dust. Reception covers exactly the building's hours, no more and no less. Waste moves against the area's collection calendar, which is not the company's calendar. Courier runs pile up and go out in rounds, not every time someone asks.

That is why the program is built around schedules before it is built around a task list: two services done well at the wrong hour get in the way just as much as one done badly.

What changes when the same team covers cleaning and complementary services?

Supervision changes, and supervision is where most of these services fall apart.

With fixed teams for each facility, the same supervisor who goes through the cleaning checklist also goes through the grounds and the holding area, and it all lands in the same report for the shift. There is no chasing three companies to find out why the garden was left half done.

The staff are the same kind of staff: on the formal payroll, with background checks and confidentiality protocols. In concierge and courier work that matters more than it does in cleaning, because those posts have access to the mail and deal directly with visitors. Staff management itself is described under staffing outsourcing.

Which waste is included in waste management and which is not?

Waste management for an office or a building covers the ordinary stream: paper, cardboard, plastic, glass, organics from the break room and the general waste of the day.

Hazardous waste, chemicals and controlled substances are not part of it. They have their own chain, in the hands of the generator or an authorized waste manager, and that holds as much in a plant as in a terminal: see industrial cleaning and ports and logistics zones.

It is worth setting out in writing, before starting, what the service removes and what stays with the client. This is the point where it most often turns out, halfway through a contract, that nobody took ownership.

What does this service not cover?

There are four things people tend to look for here that have their own page, because they are contracted differently.

Minor repairs, a door that will not close or a burned-out light, are handyman service, with staff assigned to the facility. Pest control is fumigation. Cleaning after construction or a remodel is post-construction cleaning. And if what you need is permanent cleaning staff under your own roof, that is staffing outsourcing.

If you are not sure where what you need belongs, the full catalog is under services.
