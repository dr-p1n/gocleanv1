Office cleaning

A clean workspace isn't a perk: it's productivity infrastructure.

When office cleaning fails, the team notices: internal complaints, restrooms and kitchens nobody wants to use, and that general sense of neglect that erodes morale. For companies competing for talent, the physical state of the office is part of the employee value proposition.

This page is about what is specific to an office: its size, its hours, its equipment and its documents. The daily program, with zones, frequencies and reporting, is at corporate cleaning. If you manage the building the office is in, see corporate building cleaning.

How does the type of office change what you need?

The size and use of the office decide how much cleaning it needs and when.

Small offices

A single space with a few desks, a kitchenette and a restroom. An agreed frequency and a schedule that stays out of the way matter more than a multi-shift structure.

Corporate offices on one or several floors

Reception, many workstations, meeting rooms, a kitchen or dining area and restrooms with constant traffic. Here the space is divided into zones, each with its own frequency and its own person responsible.

Coworking and flexible spaces

Different people every day, rooms booked by the hour and heavily used shared areas. They call for more attention to high-touch points and to cleaning rooms between one use and the next.

Offices that receive the public

Banks, insurers, notary offices or service centers where visitors come in all day. The reception and waiting area weigh as much as the workstations, because they are the first thing the client sees.

Is the office cleaned during the working day or outside it?

It depends on the zone. Restrooms, the kitchen and high-traffic areas need attention several times a day, while workstations and meeting rooms are best cleaned when empty, before or after hours.

Cleaning after hours means entering the office with nobody there: keys, alarm and restricted areas. That is why it is worth setting out in writing who enters, which zones and with what record. Go Services staff go through background checks and work under confidentiality protocols.

How are workstations cleaned without damaging equipment or documents?

An office desk has monitors, keyboards, phones and papers, and it is not cleaned like a floor. The working rule is not to move documents, not to spray product directly onto equipment, and to wipe the clear surfaces with barely damp cloths, treating high-touch items such as keyboards and phones separately.

It is worth agreeing with whoever is responsible for the office what to do with desks that have papers or equipment on them, so cleaning staff do not have to decide it every night.

What does replenishing sanitary supplies include?

Continuous replenishment of sanitary supplies, such as soap, paper and disinfectant, is part of the cleaning program. What is worth setting out in writing is what is replenished, how often, what the quote includes and what is billed separately.

How often is deep cleaning worthwhile?

Deep cleaning is scheduled separately from the daily service, monthly or quarterly depending on how the office is used. It covers what the daily service does not reach: carpets and upholstery, glass, corners and dust that builds up in hard-to-reach places.

For those tasks, the detail is under carpets and furniture and floor care. For dust in the office and its effect on the working environment, see dust in offices.
